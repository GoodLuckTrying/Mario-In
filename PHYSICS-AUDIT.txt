MARIO PORT PHYSICS AUDIT — 23 October 2026

The criticism is partly correct. Several ports use SMB1's constants but do not
execute its complete movement sequence. All ten installed games were reviewed.
No gameplay code or ROM was changed during this audit.

The reference is the supplied local vendor/smb1base source, with its configured
USE_SMB2J_FEATURES=0. The audit assembles verbatim movement, input filtering,
friction, jump, horizontal integration and gravity routines from player.s and
common.s. It runs those 6502 instructions against the installed port code.
Only reference variable addresses and test entry points are supplied by the
harness; movement formulas are not recreated in Python.

The six platforming comparisons total 2,724 controlled cases and 359,342
movement updates. These counts include intentionally different inputs and
host-game behavior; they are not a percentage score for each game. Mesen
controller replays separately reproduce the principal differences below.

GAME-BY-GAME FINDINGS

1. KID ICARUS
   Strongest match. All 360 expanded land cases matched, across all three forms
   and both directions, including acceleration, running, release, reversals,
   jump-speed boundaries, variable height, crouch jumps, chained jumps and
   stepping off a ledge after a fast jump. The existing 90-case/7,200-update
   test also passed, including its swimming and climbing fixtures. A fresh
   controller-driven opening jump passed in Mesen.
   This does not make Kid Icarus's terrain adapters identical to SMB1's block
   collision engine. The final stage's flight is an intentional adaptation.
   Relevant code: src/mario.s:566, :804, :876, :1046.

2. CONTRA
   Ordinary acceleration, top speeds, braking, reversals, jump forces and arcs
   match in the controlled cases. The expanded audit found input/stance and
   ledge differences: Small Mario can move with Down+Left/Right, whereas SMB1
   suppresses those horizontal inputs on the ground; tall forms crouch on the
   same combined input where SMB1's controller wrapper clears Down too.
   Leaving support resets the falling force to $70. After a fast jump, SMB1
   retains the selected $90 falling force, so the next ledge fall differs.
   Both controllers reproduced the movement traces in Mesen (2,760 updates).
   These are distinct from the intentionally retained drop-throughs, aiming,
   water-to-shore behavior and scripted Contra room transitions.
   Relevant code: contra/src/engine.inc:78, :421, :495, :862.

3. RESCUE RANGERS
   Ordinary movement and the tested jump arcs match. Crouching is cleared every
   movement update, including while airborne: a tall crouch jump becomes a
   standing-height body on its second update. SMB1 retains the crouch during
   flight. Mesen reproduced this with Mario/Super and Luigi/Fire forms.
   Down+direction filtering also differs as in Contra. Native carrying,
   throwing, box hiding, drop-throughs and object support are separate host
   mechanics and should remain available when correcting the SMB core.
   Relevant code: rangers/src/mario.s:240, :471, :487, :716.

4. KUNG FU
   Needs a substantial core correction for exact SMB1 movement. Vertical
   velocity is updated before position, every normal jump starts at -4 rather
   than selecting SMB1's speed-dependent -4/-5 launch, and the fall limit is
   different. Horizontal friction runs even with neutral airborne controls;
   animation/skid processing and timer updates occur in a different order.
   The input-normalization branch can also write facing=0 on neutral input.
   All three forms reproduced 20-pixel tap jumps and 60-pixel held jumps in
   Mesen, against SMB1's 24 and 64 pixels for the same from-rest inputs.
   A running jump reaches 60 pixels in the isolated port test against SMB1's
   80-pixel fast-jump case. Braking/reversal traces also differ.
   The native melee controls, boss barriers and staircase movement are deliberate
   integrations; they do not require keeping these ordinary movement errors.
   Relevant code: kungfu/src/mario.s:186 through :357.

5. TETRIS
   Grounded walk/run acceleration and top speeds match in the flat-floor test.
   Air control does not: friction is applied with no left/right input, and
   facing changes in the air. Jump selection happens after horizontal
   acceleration/friction rather than before it. Falling is clamped at exactly
   4.0 pixels/update instead of reproducing SMB1's fractional cap logic.
   Mesen measured a drop from 40/16 to 35/16 pixels/update during neutral air
   input, where the SMB1 reference retains 40/16. The 4.0 descent cap was also
   observed. A stationary 25-frame held jump still reaches the same 64-pixel
   apex, but its later descent differs by a pixel before landing.
   Shared piece controls, fixed well bounds, piece pushes, head breaking and
   stomps are deliberate Tetris rules. Down's soft-drop behavior should remain.
   Relevant code: tetris/src/mario.s:197, :480, :524.

6. SUPER MARIO BROS. 2 USA
   Horizontal acceleration/speed/friction values match in the existing tests,
   but the native position integrator uses magnitude-based negative movement
   and 1/16-pixel velocities. Leftward/reversal pixel timing therefore differs
   even when velocity values match. Native gravity/control/position call order
   and conversion of vertical fractions also alter the jump path.
   Mesen measured a 20-pixel one-frame tap and a 63-pixel 25-frame held jump,
   against SMB1's 24 and 64. Both measurements match the expanded CPU audit.
   SMB2's charged high jump, heart health, enemy-head standing/carrying,
   quicksand, climbing and room transitions are intentional retained mechanics.
   Relevant code: smb2/src/control.asm:2 and :154; the native normal-state
   sequence is in smb2/build/source/src/prg-0-1.asm:1961 and its integrator :2909.

7. GRADIUS
   Deliberately different movement: native horizontal flight plus custom gravity
   and hold-Up lift, with speed-capsule scaling. It does not execute SMB1's
   ground/jump controller. This matches the chosen floating/swimming design.
   Re-ran the assembled CPU checks and a Luigi gravity/lift/Options/pause replay.
   No new movement defect was established in this audit.
   Relevant code: gradius/src/mario.s:306; gradius/tests/float.lua.

8. SNAKE RATTLE 'N' ROLL
   Retains native isometric movement/momentum and changes native jump strength
   and held-A gravity. This is Mario-inspired jumping, not an exact SMB1
   trajectory or its horizontal controller. The Moon's native gravity is kept.
   Re-ran the assembled jump/gravity checks and the two-player stage-0 replay.
   No new defect beyond that known difference was established.
   Relevant code: snake/src/mario.s:321, :428 and :437.

9. THE LEGEND OF ZELDA
   Uses native four-direction overhead walking, terrain and item movement;
   there is no SMB1 ground/jump controller. The fireball speed cycle projects
   a bounce along a straight axis by design. Re-ran CPU checks and the actual
   vertical-walking/standing-recovery replay. No new movement defect was found.
   Relevant code: zelda/README.md; zelda/tools/build.py; zelda/tests/walk.lua.

10. WWF WRESTLEMANIA
   Uses native wrestling movement, running, aerial actions and opponent stats,
   with Mario/Luigi artwork and independent fireballs. It does not use SMB1
   acceleration or jumping. Re-ran CPU checks and both brothers' controller
   replays. One initial replay missed an expected melee pose; repeating the
   same-ROM replay passed, and the Luigi replay passed. This is an unresolved
   coverage-stability issue in a replay with live opponent behavior, rather
   than evidence that its retained native movement differs unexpectedly.
   Relevant code: wwf/src/mario.s:137; wwf/tests/game.lua:43.

MEASURED UPDATE SPEED

Correct formulas per movement update do not guarantee the original real-time
feel if the NES cannot finish a game update every video frame. Current-ROM
fixtures produced these measurements:

  Rescue Rangers, solo Small: 395 updates / 400 video frames (~59.3 per second).
  Rescue Rangers, solo Fire: 354 / 400 (~53.1 per second).
  Rescue Rangers, Fire co-op: 202 / 400 (~30.3 per second).
  Contra, Fire/gun co-op, stage 0: 405 / 600 (~40.5 per second).
  Contra, Fire/gun co-op, stage 6: 361 / 600 (~36.1 per second).
  Kung Fu, native-enemy stress: 4,300 / 4,300 active frames.

These are fixture measurements, not whole-campaign frame-rate guarantees or
an attribution of every CPU cycle to the mod rather than the native engine.
Rangers and Contra slowdown can make even a matching trajectory feel slower.

CORRECTION PRIORITY

First: share the exact SMB1 update sequence in Kung Fu and Tetris, including
neutral-air coasting, speed-dependent launch, fraction integration and falling
cap behavior. Preserve each game's required attacks and piece interactions.

Second: fix SMB2's velocity-to-position integration while preserving its native
collision callbacks, charged jump, carrying and heart system.

Third: repair Rescue Rangers' airborne crouch and the Contra/Rangers input
differences where they are not needed for native aiming or object actions;
preserve Contra's selected falling force across landings/ledge departures.

Separately: profile the crowded Contra and Rangers scenes. Copying constants
alone will not correct their reduced update rate.

Keep the requested Gradius float, Zelda overhead movement, Snake isometric
movement, and WrestleMania wrestling controls as explicit adaptations.

The added tests named physics-audit.lua reproduce the observed differences;
their PASS messages mean the measurement was reproduced, not that a port now
has perfect SMB1 physics. No fixes or new ROMs have been installed.

The comparison fixtures isolate ordinary movement on synthetic flat terrain,
without native enemies/camera effects unless a replay explicitly includes them.
They do not certify all collision quirks, water transitions, enemy/platform
interactions, every level, physical hardware timing or unassisted campaign play.
